What is BabyLM?

The BabyLM Challenge is a shared task and workshop on sample-efficient pretraining on a developmentally plausible corpus. Today's language models see trillions of words. A child hears fewer than 100 million words by age 13. BabyLM asks how well a model can do with a budget that small.

Teams train their own models from scratch within a fixed data budget, run them through a shared evaluation pipeline, and describe their approach in a paper for the BabyLM workshop, which is co-located with a major NLP conference (EMNLP in 2026).

Tracks in the 2026 edition

100M words

Strict

Train on a dataset of up to 100M words. You can use the official corpus or build your own.

10M words

Strict-Small

The same idea with ten times less data. A good fit for student compute budgets.

100M tokens

Multilingual

Based on BabyBabelLM, covering English, Dutch and Chinese.

Tracks and rules can change from year to year. We'll update this page once the 2027 call for papers comes out.

Roadmap

Two phases: first we learn the tools, then we compete.

Phase 1 · Nov 2026 – Feb 2027

Bootcamp

Weekly lectures, hands-on labs and paper-reading sessions. You'll train small language models from scratch, work with the BabyLM data and evaluation pipeline, and form a team with a research idea.

  • Transformer and LM training fundamentals
  • Reproduce a BabyLM baseline
  • Study past winning approaches
  • Pitch a project and form a team

Phase 2 · Feb – Jul 2027

Competition

Teams work on their BabyLM submission with support from mentors: regular check-ins, shared compute and feedback on the paper.

  • Run experiments within the data budget
  • Evaluate on the official pipeline
  • Write and submit a workshop paper
  • Submit to the leaderboard

Bootcamp curriculum

One block per month. Every week has a lecture or a lab and a reading-group session.

November

Foundations

  • Language modeling objectives: causal vs. masked LMs
  • Tokenization and the transformer architecture
  • Training a small LM from scratch with Hugging Face Transformers
  • Lab: train your first model on a 10M-word corpus

December

Data & sample efficiency

  • The BabyLM corpora: child-directed speech, children's books, dialogue
  • Data budgets, data curation and curriculum learning
  • Architectures and objectives built for little data
  • Reading group: strong submissions from past BabyLM editions

January

Evaluation

  • Targeted syntactic evaluation (BLiMP-style minimal pairs)
  • World knowledge, fine-tuning tasks and cognitively inspired benchmarks
  • Running the official BabyLM evaluation pipeline
  • Experimental design: baselines, ablations, seeds, compute budgets

February

From idea to project

  • Coming up with and scoping a research question
  • Writing a research paper in the ACL format
  • Project pitches and team formation
  • Competition kickoff 🚀

Competition phase milestones

Internal milestones for our teams. Official BabyLM deadlines will be added once they're announced for 2027.

  • Feb
    Kickoff

    Teams finalize their research proposals and get compute access.

  • Mar
    Baseline

    Every team has a working training and evaluation setup, with baseline results.

  • Apr–May
    Experiments

    Main experiments and ablations, with a mentor check-in every two weeks.

  • Jun
    Writing

    Draft the paper and get internal peer review from the other teams and the mentors.

  • Jul
    Submission

    Final paper and models submitted to BabyLM. 🎉

Who it's for

Bachelor's and master's students who want real research experience in NLP. By the end, you'll have the skills to be able to train language models from scratch, run a proper experimental study, and, we hope, co-authored a workshop paper.

Prerequisites

  • Comfortable with Python
  • Basic machine learning, linear algebra and probability
  • Some PyTorch experience (helpful, not required)

FAQ

Do I have to take part in the competition?

The bootcamp is designed to lead into the competition, and we strongly encourage everyone to stay for Phase 2.

How are teams formed?

We will organize the teams based on the parts of the common process ob building an LLM.

Do I need my own GPU?

No. BabyLM models are small by design, and we'll provide compute resources for labs and competition experiments.

Is it free?

Yes
